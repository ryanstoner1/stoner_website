I'm a [postdoctoral scholar](https://as.tufts.edu/ecs/people/postdoctoral-scholars) in
the [Department of Earth and Climate Sciences](https://as.tufts.edu/ecs) at Tufts
University, working with Dylan Vasey on how tectonic processes can be constrained by
their self-consistent evolution in numerical geodynamic models.

My work sits at the boundary between geodynamics, petrology, and numerical methods. I
build and run large-scale simulations of how the deep Earth moves and reacts — how lower
crust founders and delaminates, how water is carried into and released from a subducting
slab, and how those processes leave a record we can actually measure at the surface.

Before Tufts I was a postdoc at the University of Miami's Rosenstiel School. I completed
a PhD in Earth Science at UC Santa Barbara with Bradley Hacker, and a B.A. in Geological
Sciences at CU Boulder. Along the way I've done fieldwork in Norway, Tajikistan, the
Alps, the Bohemian Massif, and the Mojave.

I care a lot about reproducible science and open tools. Most of what I write ends up on
[GitHub](https://github.com/ryanstoner1) — including contributions to
[ASPECT](https://aspect.geodynamics.org/), the community mantle-convection code, and
[GDTchron](https://github.com/dyvasey/gdtchron), a Python package for predicting
thermochronometric ages from the output of geodynamic models.
