I'm a postdoctoral research associate at the [University of Miami Rosenstiel School of
Marine, Atmospheric, and Earth Science](https://www.earth.miami.edu/), where I model
fluid and heat transport in subduction zones with a focus on mineral phase stability.

My work sits at the boundary between geodynamics, petrology, and numerical methods. I
build and run large-scale simulations of how the deep Earth moves and reacts — how lower
crust founders and delaminates, how water is carried into and released from a subducting
slab, and how those processes leave a record we can actually measure at the surface.

Before Miami I completed a PhD in Earth Science at UC Santa Barbara with Bradley Hacker,
and a B.A. in Geological Sciences at CU Boulder. Along the way I've done fieldwork in
Norway, Tajikistan, the Alps, the Bohemian Massif, and the Mojave.

I care a lot about reproducible science and open tools. Most of what I write ends up on
[GitHub](https://github.com/ryanstoner1) — including contributions to
[ASPECT](https://aspect.geodynamics.org/), the community mantle-convection code, and
[COAST.jl](https://github.com/ryanstoner1/COAST.jl), a Julia package for sensitivity
analysis of thermal histories.
