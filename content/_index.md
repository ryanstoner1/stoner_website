I'm a [postdoctoral scholar](https://as.tufts.edu/ecs/people/postdoctoral-scholars) in
the [Department of Earth and Climate Sciences](https://as.tufts.edu/ecs) at Tufts
University, working with Dylan Vasey on how tectonic processes can be constrained by
their self-consistent evolution in numerical geodynamic models.

Before Tufts I was a postdoc at the University of Miami's Rosenstiel School, modeling
fluid and heat transport in subduction zones with a focus on mineral phase stability —
subsurface fluid dynamics and mantle dynamics with a focus on geomechanics, drawing
heavily from the tomography literature.

I completed a PhD in Earth Science at UC Santa Barbara with Bradley Hacker, and a B.A. in
Geological Sciences at CU Boulder. Along the way I've co-led international fieldwork and
collaborations in Norway and Tajikistan, along with work in the Alps, the Bohemian
Massif, and the Mojave.

I develop models to analyze fluid flow and chemical reactions, integrating publicly
available data to ensure reproducibility and accessibility. Most of what I write ends up
on [GitHub](https://github.com/ryanstoner1) — including contributions to
[ASPECT](https://aspect.geodynamics.org/), the community mantle-convection code, and
[GDTchron](https://github.com/dyvasey/gdtchron), a Python package for using the outputs of
geodynamic models to predict thermochronometric ages.
